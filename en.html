Campaign types

Four formats of influencer PR

Each format serves a different moment: a product to experience, an event to attend, a trip to live, or a presence that runs all year. Pick the one that fits where your brand is now.

Product seeding

Your product reaches hand-picked influencers as a designed first experience, from the packaging up — not just another parcel in the post.

  • Shortlisting by category, audience and city
  • Packaging and a personal note in their name
  • One polite follow-up, every post documented

Event invitations

Considered guest lists for launches, openings and events — people whose audience is actually your audience, not whoever was available.

  • Guest lists built on fit, not headcount
  • RSVP confirmation and pre-event follow-up
  • Coverage moments coordinated on the day

Brand trips

Short, programmed trips around a launch or a destination: influencers live the full experience and carry it to their audience in real time.

  • An itinerary built on postable moments
  • Guest logistics from invitation to return
  • Content collected and documented on the trip

Always-on creator relations

Not one campaign and done: working relationships all year keep your brand present at the right launches, seasons and moments.

  • A living relations base per category
  • Seasonal activations and repeat launches
  • Priority access to the right creators

Guide · Events

How to invite influencers to an event — and get them to show up

The biggest mistake in event invitations is inviting by headcount: a long list of whoever is available. Real attendance happens when every guest feels the event was built for their audience — a product that matters to them, an experience worth the trip, and respectful timing.

The invitation itself must arrive complete the first time: venue, date, what the event is, and why this person specifically. And the event itself needs moments that are ready to be filmed without anyone being asked to film.

  • ✓Invite on audience fit — not on follower count.
  • ✓Send early, remind once — late invitations turn into apologies.
  • ✓Design postable moments — an experience, not just a photo backdrop.
  • ✓Thank and document afterwards — thanks and a coverage summary build the next invitation.
Guide · Product seeding

Product seeding that actually gets posted

Seeding is not shipping products. An influencer receives plenty of parcels every week, and the one that gets posted is the one that felt like an experience made for them: a product in their category, a box that opens easily, and a short note with their name on it.

There is no posting obligation in seeding — and that is exactly its strength. A post that comes from a real experience is believed more than any agreed post, and the brands that understand this pick fewer creators, more precisely.

  • ✓Pick fewer, more precisely — category fit beats account size.
  • ✓The product shows the second the box opens — with a short personal note and one clear card.
  • ✓Tie the timing to a moment — a launch, a season, or a restock.
  • ✓Follow up once, warmly — and document every post in the coverage report.
Guide · Launches

How to brief an influencer for a product launch

A launch brief is not a long document. The influencer needs to know: what the new product is, the one thing that must reach the audience, and the facts that must never be said wrong. Everything else — style, filming, storytelling — is their craft.

A good brief protects the launch from both sides: the message does not scatter across twenty influencers, and mistakes never happen on prices or claims that are not final yet.

  • ✓One core message — if the messages multiply, nothing lands.
  • ✓A can-say / can't-say list — prices, claims and dates in their final wording.
  • ✓Posting timing tied to launch day — with a clear margin for delays.
  • ✓Usage rights agreed upfront — if the content will be reused in ads.
FAQ

What brands ask before their first PR campaign

Straight answers to the questions that come up on the first call — no detours.

What is influencer PR?

It is building a brand's presence inside influencer content through real experiences — a product they try, an event they attend, a trip they live — so they talk about it in their own voice, to an audience that trusts them, instead of a paid post with a ready-made script.

How is PR different from a paid influencer campaign?

A paid campaign buys specific posts with a set message and set timing. PR builds a relationship and an experience first, and the content comes from the influencer, in their own style. PR is stronger for credibility and reputation; paid is stronger for direct messages — many brands need both.

Does product seeding guarantee posts?

No — and no serious agency guarantees it. Seeding is an experience, not a purchased post. What we do raises the real chance of coverage: creators matched to the product's category, a carefully designed seeding experience, and timing tied to a launch or a season. When an influencer genuinely likes the product, the post carries more credibility than any ad.

Which markets do you work in?

Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the Gulf. We shortlist influencers by market, city and category, and run the sending, invitations and follow-up from one place however many markets the campaign spans.

Because every campaign is designed to fit: the number of influencers, the activation type and the market. The final price comes in a written proposal after the consultation or the brief — no generic numbers that do not match your case.
